## 1. Executive Summary

**The problem.** At the start of every semester, hundreds of thousands of Ghanaian university students stock up on food provisions — rice, oil, sardines, spaghetti, milk, Milo, gari. Today this means a crowded trip to Kejetia, Makola or a campus shop, opaque and inconsistent prices, transport costs, and carrying heavy loads back to a hostel. Parents, who very often pay for these provisions, have no reliable way to buy and deliver them from another city.

**Our solution.** Food Bundle is an online platform that sells **curated provision bundles** (ALPHA GH₵500, BETA GH₵450, GAMMA GH₵420), **fully custom bundles**, and **semester subscriptions**, delivered directly to the student's hall or hostel. Customers pay securely with Mobile Money or card via Paystack, track their delivery in real time, and confirm receipt with a secure delivery PIN. Parents can order as guests and ship nationwide.

**The opportunity.** Ghana has an estimated 600,000+ tertiary students. If each spends the price of one bundle per semester on provisions, the addressable market exceeds **GH₵500 million per year**. Our launch campuses — KNUST, University of Ghana (Legon), UPSA, ATU and UENR — represent a serviceable market of roughly **GH₵160 million per year**.

**Business model.** We earn a margin on every bundle (targeting 22% rising to 26% through bulk purchasing), plus delivery fees. Payment processing costs are passed through transparently. The platform runs on a serverless architecture with near-zero fixed technology costs.

**Why us, why now.** The full platform is already built: customer storefront, guest checkout, subscriptions, loyalty and referral programs, inventory management, packer and driver apps, and an admin dashboard. Food-delivery incumbents (Glovo, Bolt Food) focus on hot restaurant meals, and Jumia exited food delivery in 2023, leaving the **student provisions segment underserved**.

## 2. The Problem

### 2.1 For students
- **Time and effort.** A provisions trip to a central market takes half a day, often during the busiest week of the semester.
- **Transport and carrying cost.** Taxi or trotro fares plus porter fees add GH₵30–80 per trip, and heavy items (5 kg rice bags, oil, crates of eggs) are difficult to move into hostels.
- **Price opacity.** Prices vary between sellers and change without notice; students, particularly first-years, are easily overcharged.
- **Budget pressure.** Students run out of food mid-semester with no easy, affordable way to restock.

### 2.2 For parents and guardians
- Parents frequently fund provisions but live in another city.
- Current options — sending cash (which may not be spent on food) or shipping items by bus — are unreliable and offer no visibility.

### 2.3 For the market
- Existing delivery apps are optimised for **hot meals from restaurants**, not bulk dry provisions.
- Informal WhatsApp vendors exist on campuses but offer no secure payment, no tracking and inconsistent quality.

## 6. Competitive Landscape

| Alternative | Strengths | Weaknesses | Food Bundle advantage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open markets (Kejetia, Makola, etc.) | Low prices, wide choice | Time, transport, crowds, carrying, price haggling | Delivered, fixed transparent prices |
| Campus provision shops | Close by | Higher prices, limited stock, no delivery | Better prices through bulk sourcing, delivery |
| Glovo / Bolt Food / Yango | Strong brands, rider networks | Built for hot meals; costly for bulk dry goods | Purpose-built for provisions and semester plans |
| Supermarkets (online/offline) | Trusted quality | Not student-priced, limited campus delivery | Student-focused bundles and campus logistics |
| Informal WhatsApp vendors | Personal, cheap | No secure payment, tracking or consistency | Paystack security, tracking, receipts, PIN delivery |

### Sustainable competitive advantages
1. **Student-specific product design** — bundles, subscriptions and campus zones built around the academic calendar.
2. **Parent-as-payer model** — guest checkout and nationwide parcel delivery open a payer segment competitors ignore.
3. **Proprietary operating platform** — integrated inventory, packing, driver and admin tools already built; low cost to run and to extend to new campuses.
4. **Network effects through ambassadors and referrals** — built-in referral and loyalty systems turn customers into a sales channel.
5. **Procurement scale** — aggregated campus demand enables wholesaler and distributor pricing unavailable to individual students.

## 9. Operations

### 9.1 Order fulfilment flow
**Order placed & paid → admin confirms → packer assembles bundle → quality check → driver assigned → delivery with PIN confirmation → customer receipt.**

Every step is tracked in the platform with role-specific dashboards for **admins, packers, drivers and support staff**.

### 9.2 Sourcing
- Buy directly from **wholesalers and distributors** (rice, oil, canned fish, milk, beverages) and, as volume grows, negotiate manufacturer/distributor terms.
- Maintain buffer stock ahead of semester peaks; the inventory module tracks stock levels and cost per item, and bundle prices are calculated from inventory cost.

### 9.3 Packing hub
- Year 1: a small rented store/packing room near KNUST.
- Years 2–3: additional hubs in Accra and Sunyani.

### 9.4 Delivery
- Campus deliveries by contracted riders (motorbike/tricycle) on a per-delivery payout model.
- Scheduled delivery days and batching by zone keep cost per drop low.
- Inter-city orders shipped through VIP/STC parcel services for station pickup.

### 9.5 Customer support
- WhatsApp line, in-app help centre and support tickets, contact form with email follow-up.
- Clear refund policy published on the platform.

## 15. Risks & Mitigation

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Food price inflation & cedi depreciation | Margin squeeze | Inventory-driven dynamic pricing; supplier contracts; bundle composition flexibility |
| Seasonal cash-flow peaks | Stock-outs or idle cash | Pre-orders and subscriptions ahead of reopening; supplier credit; working-capital reserve |
| Low initial adoption | Slower growth | Ambassador and referral incentives; launch promotions; parent-targeted marketing |
| Delivery reliability | Poor customer experience | Zone batching, scheduled delivery days, PIN confirmation, rider performance tracking |
| Food safety & expiry | Reputational and legal | FIFO stock rotation, expiry tracking, reputable suppliers, FDA-registered products only |
| Competition from large delivery apps | Price pressure | Focus on provisions, subscriptions and the parent payer — a segment they don't target |
| Payment fraud | Financial loss | Paystack verification with signed webhooks; orders confirmed only after verified payment |
| Data protection & regulation | Legal | Database-level access control; compliance with the Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843); published privacy and refund policies |
| Key-person dependency | Operational | Documented processes in the platform; role-based tools; early hiring of an operations lead |
